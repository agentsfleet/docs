## What it is

A workspace groups fleets, secrets, connectors, and access. Each workspace belongs to one tenant.

A workspace can have fleets for many repositories. Each fleet has its own triggers.

## Why it exists

Use separate workspaces when teams or environments need different fleets and secrets. Billing and provider settings remain at the tenant level.

## How it behaves

Create a workspace through the client or API. The dashboard calls its owning tenant an account.

An account has one owner, the person who signed up. The owner can invite members, who open and steer every workspace in the account. Only the owner stores secrets, connects integrations, and manages people.

A fleet belongs to exactly one workspace. Secrets and connectors do not cross workspace boundaries.

Account setup creates a default workspace.
After login, the client keeps your previous workspace if you can still reach it. Otherwise it selects the first available workspace.

## Limits

`agentsfleet` does not publish a workspace count limit. Service limits still apply to work inside each workspace.

The client has no remote workspace removal call. `workspace delete` only removes one workspace from local client state.
